# The rubric — scoring a piece (and the baseline)

Six dimensions, 0–2 each, 12 total. Score the artifact in front of you,
not the intention behind it. Anchor statements are literal: if the
anchor's test fails, the point is not earned.

## Dimensions

**1. Intuition-first order**
- 2: A manipulation and a committed prediction both occur before the
  first definition or formula appears.
- 1: Manipulation comes first, but formalism slips in before any
  committed prediction — or the opening "manipulation" is decorative.
- 0: The piece opens with a definition, formula, or exposition.

**2. Manipulability**
- 2: The learner directly moves the concept's state (drag/step/turn a
  true parameter) and the concept's behavior answers truthfully across
  the reachable range, including an extreme.
- 1: Interaction exists but is shallow (preset buttons only, or a
  range too narrow to reach interesting states).
- 0: No interaction; a static explanation or a watch-only animation.

**3. Misconception handling**
- 2: At least one named misconception is stated in the learner's terms
  and visibly broken by a reachable figure state; the path says what
  the failure reveals.
- 1: A misconception is mentioned but never made to fail in the
  learner's hands.
- 0: No misconception is addressed; the piece only presents correct
  material.

**4. Zero hand-drill**
- 2: No instruction asks the learner to compute anything by hand; all
  numbers are model-produced and the learner's work is prediction,
  interpretation, explanation.
- 1: Mostly clean, but one exercise slips in that requires hand
  calculation to complete.
- 0: The piece contains drills, problem sets, or worked arithmetic the
  learner must reproduce.

**5. Transfer to AI**
- 2: The concept is shown inside one concrete AI system, naming the
  exact place it sits, plus a "spot it in the wild" prompt.
- 1: Applications are listed generically ("used in neural networks")
  without a concrete seat for the concept.
- 0: No transfer; the concept stays inside mathematics.

**6. Plain-language defensibility**
- 2: A Defend beat exists whose questions cannot be answered by
  quoting the piece, plus closed-book recall prompts (one-sentence
  intuition, the trap, the symbols' meanings).
- 1: Comprehension questions exist but can be answered by copying a
  sentence from the piece.
- 0: No check of understanding at all.

## Protocol

1. Score the finished piece. **Ship threshold: ≥ 9/12 and no zero.**
2. For the behavioral comparison, produce the baseline under comparable
   conditions: same agent, same concept, same rough length/effort, a
   plain request with no skill ("explain X so I understand it").
   Score both with this rubric, dimension by dimension, with the
   deciding evidence quoted or pointed at (a line, a state, a missing
   element) — not vibes.
3. Record both score tables and the per-dimension evidence in the
   behavioral test doc. A tie or loss is a finding, not an
   embarrassment: it feeds the honesty ledger, and the skill is
   iterated or its claims downgraded.
4. Known bias, declared: the scorer is usually the author. Mitigate by
   scoring the baseline *first* and writing the baseline before
   finalizing the piece where practical; state the limitation in the
   test doc regardless.
